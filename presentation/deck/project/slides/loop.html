07 · THE CORE DIFFERENTIATOR

Detection is not the end. Neither is “training completed”.

Most systems stop at detection, or at “training completed”.

We measure behaviour after training, verify it statistically, and escalate to a coach when it didn't work.

A supervisor reviews each completed training before monitoring starts. Approval is not verification.

SAY (60 s, slow down, this is the climax): "This is the heart of the project. Most systems stop at detection: here's your idle percentage. Some go one step further and assign training. And then they tick 'training completed' and stop. We don't. Step one, we observe every session. Two, we score it against a written standard, for example 'idle at most 20% of the session'. A competency score is simply the share of your last 30 sessions that met the standard. Three, below 70 is a skill gap. Four, the gap picks a matched lesson plus a three-question quiz, and a supervisor reviews the completed training. Five, and this is the difference: after training we compare the operator's later sessions with the ones before, statistically. Six, we adapt: verified, keep monitoring, or escalate to an instructor if nothing changed. The question the system asks is not 'did they finish the course', but 'did the operator actually improve?'" KNOW: 6 competencies: Safety discipline, Fuel efficiency, Cycle efficiency, Machine handling, Terrain handling (slopes ≥ 5° only), Task execution (vs out-of-fold model time; coaching, no lesson). Score needs ≥ 8 applicable sessions in the last 30. Gap < 70. Pass mark 2/3 (≥ 66%). Standards are explicit policy choices, not learned. Ravi's terrain = 31/100 (16 slope sessions: 4 with tilt > 15°, 5 with > 7° above slope, 9 over 95% of the speed limit). IF ASKED "Why 70?": "It's an explicit, adjustable policy threshold, not a learned value; a site safety manager would set it."
