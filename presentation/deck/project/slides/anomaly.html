SAY (50 s): "For unusual machine usage we use an Isolation Forest. In simple words: it keeps splitting the data at random, and sessions that get isolated in very few splits are the strange ones. We feed it six signals per session, normalised within each machine type so an excavator isn't compared with a truck, and flag the 5% most unusual. But a score alone isn't useful to an operator, so every flagged session gets evidence: this session's idle share was 59%, versus 19% for this operator normally and 13% for the site, plus a recommendation. How good is it? Because our data is synthetic, we know where we injected anomalies: it catches 80% of the idle and fuel anomalies, with 61.5% precision. It's weak on speed-only spikes, 13.6%, and we say so." SAY IT EXACTLY: "80% recall on injected idle/fuel anomalies". Never "80% accuracy". KNOW: IsolationForest(contamination=0.05, random_state=0), 100 trees; robust z = (x − median) / (1.4826 × MAD) within machine type. Reason text = top 2 signals with |z| ≥ 2. Evidence table = top 3 signals by deviation from the site median (operator and site medians exclude the session itself). Runs once at startup on history; not live, not persisted, doesn't feed training. In-app "flags with a known cause 75.6%" is NOT precision. Known quirk: the one-line reason and the evidence ranking can highlight different signals. IF ASKED "Why not supervised?": "Real incidents are rarely labelled. Unsupervised plus evidence is the practical start; with labelled incidents you'd validate or move to supervised."
