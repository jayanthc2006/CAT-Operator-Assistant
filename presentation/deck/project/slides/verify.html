08 · HOW WE VERIFY TRAINING

We don't count training completion as success

Fisher exact test

Did the share of sessions meeting the standard go up?

one-sided · pass/fail counts, before vs after

Mann–Whitney U

Did the key behaviour metric shift in the right direction?

one-sided · e.g. idle share, unbuckled share

  • Verified: p < 0.05, in the right direction
  • Monitoring: needs ≥ 8 post-training sessions
  • Escalate: 15 sessions, no improvement → coach
  • Supervisor approval starts monitoring, never Verified

SAY (55 s): "Here's how verification works. We take the operator's 30 sessions before the training and up to 30 after. Two one-sided tests: Fisher's exact test asks whether the share of sessions meeting the standard went up; Mann–Whitney asks whether the behaviour metric itself, say idle share, moved the right way. Verified means p below 0.05 in the right direction. We need at least eight sessions after training before judging; before that it's 'monitoring'. After 15 sessions with no improvement, it escalates to an instructor. On our seeded synthetic history: Ravi's fuel efficiency went from 27% to 80% of sessions meeting the standard, verified. Anita's safety discipline, 60 to 93%, verified. Priya's fuel training didn't change anything, so the system escalates her to coaching instead of claiming success. To be clear: these effects were seeded in synthetic data, and with no control group this is before/after evidence, not causality." KNOW: Ravi idle share 26.6% → 15.5% (MW p ≈ 6e-7; Fisher p ≈ 3.6e-5). Anita unbuckled 33% → 0% (MW p ≈ 0.0003). Priya idle 26.7% → 24.2% (MW p ≈ 0.30). Why Fisher: small counts (8–30), where chi-square is unreliable. Why Mann–Whitney: skewed metrics, no normality assumption. No multiple-testing correction. !!
