SAY (50 s): "The planner estimates a task from a standard rate and soil type. Our model, a gradient-boosting regressor, uses ten inputs that are all known before the task starts: task type, weather, skill, machine age and type, soil, temperature, slope, volume, and the operator's own past pace from earlier days. We evaluated it honestly: trained on the first 1,238 sessions and tested on 311 later sessions it had never seen. On that held-out synthetic test set, mean absolute error drops from 24.3 minutes to 12.3: 49.5% lower, with a bootstrap confidence interval of 43.5 to 54.9%, and 40 to 55% across five different generator seeds. 42% of tasks land within ±10% of the actual time, versus 15% for the planner." SAY IT EXACTLY: "49.5% lower MAE than the planner on our held-out synthetic test set." NOT "49.5% more accurate". KNOW: Temporal split = first 80% of days train (10 Jul – 7 Sep), last 20% test (8 – 22 Sep) for a DB created 23 Sep; dates shift with the reset day. Task-type-median baseline MAE 30.70 min. Oracle (generator's own observable formula) 10.21 min, so the rest is unobservable noise. Bias: model −1.7 min, planner −24.0 min. Hyperparameters: max_iter 300, lr 0.06, min_samples_leaf 20. Global importance: volume 40.5%, task type 38.4%, soil 10.3%. Explanations are substitution-based and indicative, not causal. No per-prediction uncertainty: one global ±12 min.
