03 · IN-CAB HMI

Designed around the operator

HMI = Human–Machine Interface: the operator-facing screen, intended for inside the cab.

  • Pre-start inspection gate (8/8 checks)
  • Current task + model ETA
  • NORMAL / CAUTION / CRITICAL state
  • Safety alerts with a “Why”
  • 360° proximity radar + critical telemetry
  • Fatigue-risk indicator (not medical)
  • Site conditions + one-tap training action

◌ Prototype: runs locally (Streamlit) on a laptop/tablet with simulated telemetry. Not connected to a CAT machine.

SAY (45 s): "HMI means human–machine interface: the screen the operator actually looks at in the cab. This is our Live Cab. Before the shift, the app stays locked until the 8-point walkaround inspection is complete. Then the operator sees one task card with the model's ETA, and one big state: NORMAL, CAUTION or CRITICAL. Here we've triggered two simulated events: a worker 1.6 metres away and a 23-degree tilt. Each red banner says what's wrong, why, and what to do, and the tilt alert links straight to a 42-second slope lesson. Below that: the 360-degree proximity radar, critical telemetry and a fatigue-risk indicator. Note the 'simulated telemetry' chip: this is a prototype on a laptop, not a real machine." KNOW: Telemetry refreshes every 2 s (Streamlit fragment). Readings come from data.live_reading (simulated). Fatigue-risk indicator = rule-based points for time on shift (>6 h), time since break (>2 h), heat (>32 °C) and alerts this shift; it's an operational indicator, NOT a medical measure. The checklist lock is app-level only. IF ASKED "Is the machine really locked?": "No: the app gates the Live Cab until the inspection is complete. A physical interlock would have to go through certified machine safety systems; that's on the roadmap."
